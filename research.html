01The question

When a curve feels wrong,
what exactly is happening?

Zoom in on the outline of a logo, a letter, or an icon. The anchor points have been carefully considered, and the handles appear to be aligned correctly. And yet the line seems to bulge ever so slightly. Manual correction often fixes one area only to make another thinner, heavier, or throw off the balance of the whole shape. The challenge of editing Bézier curves is to articulate what feels unnatural, preserve the designer’s intent, and change only what is safe to change.

Our working thesisQuantify the discomfort.
Protect the design intent.
02Continuity

Smoothness
is not one thing.

Two curves can meet at the same point and still produce a visible “step.” We investigate the levels of continuity behind the word “smooth.”

Signed curvatureκ(t) = cross(B′(t), B″(t)) / |B′(t)|³

We compare how much each side bends—including the direction of that bending—not as a raw second-derivative value, but as geometric continuity at the connection.

03Fairness

A correct junction
is not a beautiful line.

Even when both sides match perfectly at the connection, distortion or a small ripple can remain somewhere along the curve. We distinguish local continuity from the natural fairness of the entire line.

E_fair = ∫(dκ/ds)² ds

Curvature-change energy. A measure that penalizes abrupt changes in curvature. It is a guide, not an absolute answer: the mathematically smoothest curve is not necessarily the one that preserves the brand’s character.

05Perception

Not “0.01 units.”
How many pixels move?

The meaning of a coordinate difference changes with the final display size. A 0.1-unit change on a 1000-unit artboard becomes meaningful only after it is converted into a distance people can see.

16 pxICON
256 pxSCREEN

Evaluate maximum displacement in the pixels people will actually see. A small residual may be harmless at one size and a critical flaw at another.

DETECTION PRIORITYPrioritize perceptual impact
over absolute residual size.

We will vary display size, contrast, stroke weight, and output medium to identify where mathematical metrics align with expert judgement.

06Illustrator

09Conclusion

Do not decide the
right line for people.

harmonism line is not meant to replace the designer’s eye. It is a tool for making discomfort measurable, showing what changed and what was preserved, and making experimentation safe. Equations are only the starting point. The real research lies at the boundary between mathematics, cognition, and interaction—without compromising Illustrator’s existing workflow.
